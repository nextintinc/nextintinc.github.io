Learn the logic, move sequence strategies, and perimeter peeling rules to master tricky levels in Tap Arrow: Tap Away Puzzle.

At first glance, arrow tap-away logic games seem deceptively simple: each arrow points in a cardinal direction (Up, Down, Left, or Right). You tap it, and it swoops off the screen. 1. The Golden Rule: The Outside-In Peeling Technique

The number one mistake beginner players make is tapping arrows positioned in the middle of the board. Central arrows are almost always obstructed by multiple outer barriers.

Instead, adopt the Perimeter Peeling Method:

  • Scan the outer perimeter: Look along the top, bottom, left, and right borders of the arrow cluster.
  • Identify outward-facing arrows: Any arrow on the outer edge pointing directly outward toward empty screen space has zero obstruction. It is 100% safe to tap.
  • Peel layer by layer: Once the outermost layer is cleared, previously interior arrows become the new perimeter. Continue peeling toward the center.

2. Visualizing Vector Trajectories (Avoid Phantom Paths)

Arrows don't just clear their immediate neighboring cell — they fly in a straight beam across the entire grid. Even if an arrow's direct neighbor is empty, an arrow located 4 blocks away in the same line of sight will block its flight.

Before tapping, let your eyes scan the full line across the board:

  1. Locate the arrow tip.
  2. Follow its ray until it exits the screen boundary.
  3. Verify that zero arrows intersect that ray.
  4. Tap with confidence!

💡 Pro Tip: Identify "Keystone" Arrows

A "keystone" arrow is one that holds multiple other arrows hostage. Often, finding just one tricky unblocked keystone triggers an explosive domino reaction, allowing 5 to 10 surrounding arrows to fly away effortlessly.

3. Detecting Mutual Blockages (Arrow Standoffs)

A common puzzle architecture in higher levels of Tap Arrow is the Mutual Standoff: Arrow A points Right at Arrow B, while Arrow B points Down at Arrow C, and Arrow C points Left.

In circular dependencies, none of the inner cycle can escape until the anchor block is released from the outside. If you see two arrows pointing directly into each other tip-to-tip, neither can move until at least one perpendicular lane opens up. Never try to force an arrow through a collision point.

4. When to Use Hints, Undo, and Revives Strategically

Tap Arrow includes thoughtful quality-of-life tools to preserve your flow state rather than creating frustration:

  • The Undo Button: Made an accidental tap or realized an arrow uncovered a dead end? Tap Undo immediately. It restores your previous position without any point penalty.
  • Smart Hints: If you've stared at a board for more than 45 seconds without finding a free path, use a Hint. It gently illuminates an arrow that currently has an unobstructed flight corridor, helping you train your pattern recognition for similar setups.
  • Daily Streaks: Playing just 5 minutes a day keeps your problem-solving reflexes sharp and climbs the global leaderboard rankings.

5. Practice Makes Perfect

Like chess or Sudoku, your spatial anticipation improves rapidly with practice. You will begin to spot unobstructed angles intuitively, predicting two and three moves ahead before your finger even touches the glass.
